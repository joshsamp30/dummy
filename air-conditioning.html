AC Repair & Maintenance

Air conditioners can fail in dozens of ways, but a small handful of issues account for the vast majority of summer service calls. The most common are refrigerant leaks (often from corroded copper lines or a failing schrader valve), failed dual-run capacitors, dirty condenser coils restricting heat rejection, frozen evaporator coils caused by low airflow, and contactors that have welded shut. Each of these has a different repair cost and a different urgency. Our technicians explain what's wrong in plain English, give you the price up front, and have the parts on the truck for nearly every common repair.

Routine air conditioner maintenance prevents most of these failures from ever happening. A proper AC tune-up — not the 15-minute "filter swap and leave" some companies offer, but a full inspection — should include: coil cleaning, refrigerant pressure check, electrical-component testing, blower-wheel cleaning, condensate-line clearing, thermostat calibration, and a check of all safety controls. We perform tune-ups on every brand and recommend annual service in spring before the cooling season starts. Customers on our Peak Performance Maintenance Plan get priority dispatch, no overtime charges and discounted parts.

Signs Your AC Needs Repair

  • Warm air blowing from the vents when the system is running
  • Unusual noises — rattling, screeching, banging or clicking
  • Ice forming on refrigerant lines or the indoor coil
  • An electric bill that has jumped without a usage change
  • The system runs constantly but never reaches the set temperature
  • Strong musty or burning smells coming from the registers
  • Water pooling around the indoor air handler or in the basement
  • The breaker trips when the AC starts or the unit short-cycles

AC Installation & Replacement

If your air conditioner is over 10 years old, has needed multiple repairs in recent seasons, or uses the now-phased-out R-22 refrigerant, replacement almost always saves money over time. Modern systems with SEER2 ratings of 16+ can use 30–50% less electricity than units installed before 2010. We install central air conditioners, heat pumps, ductless mini-splits and high-efficiency hybrid systems from every major manufacturer including Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Bryant, Mitsubishi and WaterFurnace.

Proper sizing is the single biggest factor in how well your new system will perform — and it's also the area where many contractors cut corners. We perform a full Manual J load calculation on every replacement, accounting for square footage, insulation R-values, window orientation, ductwork condition and air leakage. An oversized unit short-cycles, fails to dehumidify, and wears out faster. An undersized unit runs constantly and never quite reaches the set point. The right size, installed correctly, will outlast its warranty by years.

Ductless Mini-Split AC Systems

Ductless mini-splits are the right answer for older homes without ductwork, room additions, finished basements, garages, sunrooms, and any application where running ductwork would be expensive or impossible. They're also remarkably efficient — modern inverter-driven mini-splits deliver SEER2 ratings up to 30+ and can heat or cool individual rooms (zones) independently, which dramatically reduces wasted energy. We install single-zone and multi-zone mini-splits that connect up to 8 indoor heads to a single outdoor condenser.

Air Handler Services

The air handler is the indoor half of your cooling system. Inside it sits the evaporator coil, the blower motor, the air filter and (often) an electric backup heat strip. When the air handler fails, the whole system fails. We service blower motors, ECM modules, evaporator coils, condensate pans and primary/secondary float switches that protect your home from water damage when the drain line clogs.

FAQ — Air Conditioning

How often should AC be serviced?

Once a year, in spring, before the cooling season. Annual maintenance preserves manufacturer warranties on most equipment and catches small problems before they cause expensive failures.

What temperature should I set my thermostat to?

78°F when home, 82°F when away is the sweet spot for cost-and-comfort balance. Every degree below 78 typically increases cooling cost 6–8%.

Why does my AC keep freezing up?

Almost always either low refrigerant (a leak) or restricted airflow (dirty filter, dirty coil or a closed return). Turn the system off immediately when you see ice — running a frozen AC damages the compressor.

Should I cover my outdoor unit in winter?

Generally no. Outdoor condensers are built for the weather. A full cover traps moisture and invites rodents. A small piece of plywood weighted on top is fine if you're worried about falling debris.
