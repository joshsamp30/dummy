How Geothermal Works

The temperature six feet below the surface of your yard stays remarkably stable year-round — somewhere between 50°F and 60°F in our region — regardless of whether it's 100°F or –10°F outside. Geothermal systems use a closed loop of polyethylene pipe buried in your yard to circulate water (or water-and-antifreeze) and exchange heat with the earth. In winter, the system extracts heat from the ground and pumps it indoors. In summer, the cycle reverses: the system pulls heat from inside your home and rejects it into the cooler ground. Because moving heat is dramatically more efficient than creating heat, geothermal systems run on a fraction of the electricity required by traditional electric or gas heating.

There are three primary loop configurations. Vertical loops are drilled 200–400 feet straight down and are ideal for properties with limited yard space. Horizontal loops are buried 4–6 feet deep across your property and are typically the most economical when space allows. Pond loops use a body of water on your property and are the cheapest option when a pond of sufficient size and depth is available. We design every system around your specific lot, soil conditions, water table and household heating/cooling load.

Geothermal Installation Process

A typical geothermal installation takes 1–2 weeks from drilling start to commissioning, depending on loop type, soil conditions and weather. Here's what to expect:

  1. Site assessment & load calculation. We perform a Manual J calculation, evaluate soil thermal conductivity, assess your yard for loop placement, and review your existing ductwork.
  2. System design. Loop length, heat-pump tonnage and indoor-equipment sizing are calculated specifically for your home.
  3. Drilling or trenching. Our crew installs the loop field — vertical bores or horizontal trenches — over 1–4 days.
  4. Indoor installation. The heat pump is installed in the basement or mechanical room and connected to your existing or new ductwork.
  5. Loop connection & flushing. The loop is filled with water (and antifreeze where required), pressure-tested, and flushed to remove any air or debris.
  6. Commissioning & testing. The system is started, calibrated, tested in heating and cooling modes, and registered for warranty.

Tax Credits, Rebates & Financing

The Inflation Reduction Act extended the federal residential geothermal tax credit through 2032 at 30% of total system cost — including installation, drilling and tax. There is no cap on the credit. On a typical $30,000–$45,000 geothermal installation, that's $9,000–$13,500 back at tax time. Many Missouri homeowners stack this credit with utility rebates from Evergy and Spire and with our flexible Optimus financing (apply with a soft credit check that doesn't ding your score) to make geothermal cost-competitive with conventional equipment from day one.

Geothermal Repair & Maintenance

Geothermal systems require remarkably little maintenance compared to conventional HVAC equipment. There's no combustion to manage, no outdoor unit exposed to weather, no flame sensor to clean. The most common service items are loop pressure checks, fluid balancing, filter changes, blower-motor service and occasional pump replacement. We service every brand of geothermal heat pump including WaterFurnace, ClimateMaster, Bosch, Carrier, Trane and Hydron Module.

Geothermal FAQ

How much can I really save with geothermal?

Most Kansas City homes see 60–90% savings on heating and cooling costs versus electric resistance heat or older gas/AC systems. The exact number depends on insulation, household load and what you're replacing. We'll model expected savings for your home before you commit.

How long does the loop last?

The polyethylene loop pipe is rated for 50+ years; many will last beyond that. The indoor heat pump typically lasts 20–25 years.

Will geothermal work in my yard?

Almost certainly. Vertical bores fit on lots of any size; horizontal loops need more yard but cost less. Even small urban lots usually accommodate a system.

Is geothermal noisy?

The loop is silent — it's underground. The indoor unit runs at a level similar to a quiet refrigerator. There is no outdoor compressor, so neighbours don't hear anything.
