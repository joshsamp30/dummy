Why Go Tankless? Benefits of Switching to a Tankless Water Heater

Energy efficiency, endless hot water, space savings and lifespan — a long look at why on-demand water heaters are taking over.

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Why Go Tankless?

Modernising a home's plumbing infrastructure often involves reevaluating how essential resources are delivered. As Kansas City homeowners seek ways to improve functionality and reduce their carbon footprint, the limitations of older technology become increasingly apparent. Moving away from bulky, traditional reservoirs in favor of a tankless water heater installation represents a smart evolution in home maintenance. This shift not only addresses immediate comfort needs but also serves as a proactive step toward a more sustainable and cost-effective household.

Tankless water heater install

Energy Efficiency

The most significant advantage of switching to tankless lies in its impressive energy efficiency. Conventional units, known as storage water heaters, must keep a large volume of water hot 24 hours a day, regardless of whether anyone is home to use it. This constant reheating leads to "standby loss," in which heat steadily radiates from the hot water tank, wasting fuel and driving up monthly expenses.

In contrast, a tankless water heater operates on a strictly on-demand basis. It uses high-powered burners or electric coils to flash-heat water only when a tap is opened. Because the system does not waste energy keeping a reserve tank hot when effectively idle, it consumes significantly less power. This efficient operation directly translates into lower utility bills than with a standard storage-tank water heater — typically 24–34% lower for households with moderate hot-water use.

Endless Hot Water Supply

One of the most common frustrations with a traditional storage water heater is running out of hot water at inconvenient times. Once the pre-heated supply is drained — perhaps after a few back-to-back showers, or while running a dishwasher and washing machine simultaneously — the household is forced to wait for the tank to refill and recover temperature.

A tankless unit solves this logistical problem by heating water instantaneously as it passes through the pipes. This provides a virtually endless supply, ensuring hot water is available whenever needed, without the fear of a cold shower mid-rinse. As long as the unit is properly sized for your home's flow demand, you can run multiple fixtures simultaneously without temperature drop.

Space Savings

Beyond the functional benefits, the physical design of tankless models offers a major spatial advantage. A standard hot water tank is a large, cylindrical appliance that dominates valuable floor space in a basement or utility closet — typically 20" wide by 60" tall. Conversely, tankless units are compact, often the size of a carry-on suitcase, and are mounted on the wall. This space-saving design eliminates the footprint of a large vessel, freeing up the area for extra storage, a workshop or even a finished room.

Long Lifespan

When considering the long-term value of a water heater replacement, durability is a key differentiator. Traditional storage units are prone to rust and sediment buildup because they hold water continuously, which accelerates corrosion. As a result, these systems typically need to be replaced every 8–12 years.

Tankless systems, however, are not subject to the constant wear of holding standing water. Because they are designed with replaceable components and cleaner operation, a tankless water heater can last upwards of 20 years with routine maintenance. This extended lifespan means homeowners can delay the expense and hassle of their next replacement, offering a better return on investment over time.

Maintenance Requirements

Tankless units do require routine maintenance to deliver their full lifespan. Annual descaling — flushing the heat exchanger with vinegar or a manufacturer-approved descaler — is essential, especially in Kansas City's hard-water region. Without descaling, mineral buildup gradually reduces flow rate and efficiency. Our service technicians perform descaling as part of routine plumbing maintenance.

Sizing Matters

The biggest mistake homeowners make with tankless is undersizing. A tankless unit is rated by gallons-per-minute (GPM) at a specific temperature rise. In Kansas City, where incoming groundwater can be as cold as 45°F in winter, a temperature rise of 75°F (to deliver 120°F hot water) is typical. The right unit for a 4-bedroom home is generally 8–10 GPM at that rise; smaller units will struggle on cold mornings. We size every installation properly using actual demand data and incoming water temperature.
