Safety

Why is it important to test for chimney gas leaks?

Your chimney could be the source of a gas leak. Gas leaks aren't common, but they are dangerous. We use professional combustible-gas analysers that detect leaks utility-grade detectors miss. Annual chimney and flue inspection is part of our heating tune-up.

Do we need a carbon monoxide detector?

Yes. CO is colourless and odourless. It's especially dangerous to sleeping or intoxicated occupants who can't recognise the symptoms. We recommend a low-level CO monitor (sensitive to lower concentrations than typical retail models) on every floor of every home with combustion appliances or an attached garage.

What are the dangers of mold in construction and remodeling?

Mold breaks down surfaces and releases dust mites, bacteria, chemicals, gases and other pollutants into the air. During remodeling, sealed building cavities can quickly become mold breeding grounds. Proper ventilation and humidity control during and after construction are essential.

Indoor Air Quality

Air quality — what are we breathing?

Indoor air can be 2–5x more polluted than outdoor air, and most of us spend 90% of our time indoors. Pollutants directly affect lung health, sleep, cognition and allergy symptoms. Visit our indoor air quality page for solutions we install daily.

What are the health benefits of a humidifier?

Indoor humidity below 30% causes dry skin, irritated sinuses, cracked wood furniture and increased static. A whole-home humidifier maintains 30–50% RH year-round and protects both your health and your home.

How often should I change my air filter?

Every 30 days for 1-inch filters, every 60–90 days for pleated, every 6–12 months for 4–5-inch media filters. Set a phone reminder. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of furnace and AC breakdowns we see.

What is the acceptable indoor air quality level?

Targets per EPA, WHO and ASHRAE: PM2.5 below 12 µg/m³ annual average; CO2 generally 800–1,200 ppm; total VOCs below 500 µg/m³; formaldehyde below 100 µg/m³ over 30 minutes; CO below 9 ppm over 8 hours; humidity 30–50%; temperature 68–78°F.

How can I test my indoor air quality?

Consumer monitors detect particulates, VOCs, CO2, humidity and temperature. For comprehensive testing — including formaldehyde, radon and mold — hire a professional. We offer professional IAQ testing as part of our energy audits.

Can poor indoor air quality make you sick?

Yes. Symptoms include headaches, fatigue, shortness of breath, sinus congestion, eye/nose/throat/skin irritation, allergic reactions and asthma attacks. Long-term exposure is linked to respiratory disease, heart disease and cancer.

What is Toxic House Syndrome?

Toxic House (or Sick Building) Syndrome describes the constellation of symptoms occupants experience when indoor air is contaminated and the source is unknown. Common causes include VOCs, poor ventilation, dust and pet dander, mold, tobacco smoke, pesticides and combustion gases.

HVAC Systems & Maintenance

Glossary of HVAC terms?

HVAC pros use industry shorthand that can sound like another language. We maintain a glossary of HVAC terms to help homeowners understand quotes and diagnostics. Common terms: AFUE (furnace efficiency), SEER2 (cooling efficiency), MERV (filter efficiency), CFM (airflow), tonnage (cooling capacity).

How do I find the best HVAC contractor?

Look for: state and local licensing, NATE certification, BBB rating, online reviews, manufacturer warranties on installations, written estimates, and the ability to pull permits. Avoid contractors who quote over the phone without seeing the home.

How do I maintain HVAC equipment?

Annual professional tune-ups (spring for AC, fall for heating), monthly filter changes, keep outdoor unit clear of debris, schedule duct inspection every few years, address unusual noises or smells immediately. Customers on our Peak Performance Maintenance Plan get priority dispatch and discounted parts.

How does a furnace work?

A furnace pulls cold return air across a heat exchanger that's heated by burning natural gas (or by electric heat strips). The blower then pushes the warmed air through your supply ducts to every room. Modern condensing furnaces extract additional heat from exhaust gases for efficiency up to 98% AFUE.

How does central air conditioning work?

An AC moves heat from inside your home to outside using refrigerant. Indoor warm air passes over a cold evaporator coil; the refrigerant absorbs the heat and carries it outside, where the condenser releases it. The cold air is recirculated back into your home.

Benefits of furnace maintenance?

Higher efficiency, lower gas bills, fewer breakdowns, longer equipment life, valid manufacturer warranty and verified safety (combustion analysis catches CO problems early).

Air conditioning maintenance and tune-ups?

Coils get dirty, belts slip, refrigerant leaks slowly, capacitors degrade. An annual AC tune-up restores capacity and efficiency, prevents most failures and pays for itself in the first season.

How often should I clean my HVAC system?

Filters monthly. Coils annually (part of a tune-up). Ducts every 5–10 years. Whole-system deep clean if you've had recent construction, water damage, pet hoarding or rodent intrusion.

Mini Split Heat Pumps

Why does a heat pump blow cold air?

A heat pump's supply air is typically 90–105°F — warmer than room temperature but cooler than a gas furnace's 130–140°F. That's normal. If air is actually cold (below room temp), the system is in defrost mode or there's a refrigerant problem.

What temperature should I set my thermostat?

Personal preference, but: 68°F when home in winter, 60°F when sleeping/away. 78°F home in summer, 82°F away. Every degree closer to outdoor temperature saves 6–8% on conditioning costs.

What is a mini split heat pump? How does it work?

A mini split heat pump extracts heat from outdoor air during winter and transfers it indoors; the cycle reverses for cooling in summer. It's a refrigerant-based system using a compressor, condenser, expansion valve and evaporator. Outdoor and indoor units are connected by refrigerant lines and electrical cables.

Are all mini splits heat pumps?

No. "Mini split" describes the form factor (an outdoor and one or more indoor units, ductless). A "heat pump" mini split can both heat and cool. Some mini splits are cooling-only. If you want year-round comfort, ask specifically for a heat pump model.

Are mini split heat pumps efficient?

Very. Modern inverter-driven mini splits deliver SEER2 ratings of 20–30+ and HSPF ratings of 10+. Zoning further reduces energy use because you only condition the rooms you're using.

Benefits of a mini split heat pump?

No ductwork required, easy installation in retrofits, energy efficiency, dual heating/cooling, quiet operation (compressor is outside), zoning capability, eligible for federal heat-pump tax credit up to $2,000.

Life expectancy of a mini split heat pump?

15–20 years with proper installation and regular maintenance. Filter cleaning, refrigerant level checks, and outdoor-unit inspection extend life significantly.
