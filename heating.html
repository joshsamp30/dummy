Heating systems wear out the same way every year — slowly, then all at once. The early warnings are subtle: a slightly higher gas bill, an unfamiliar smell, an extra few seconds of run time per cycle. By the time the unit fails to start on the coldest morning of the year, it has usually been telling you something for months. Our recommendation to every customer is simple: schedule a tune-up every fall, replace your furnace filter on the schedule the manufacturer recommends, and call us at the first sign of anything unusual. The cost of catching a problem early is almost always a fraction of the cost of replacing the equipment that the problem destroyed.

Furnace Repair & Installation

Gas furnaces remain the most common heating system in the Kansas City metro because natural gas is plentiful, the equipment is reliable, and modern condensing furnaces deliver up to 98% AFUE efficiency. We service every brand and most models from the last 30 years. Common furnace repairs we handle include flame sensor cleaning, ignitor replacement, blower motor and capacitor replacement, gas valve replacement, control board diagnosis, draft inducer service, and heat exchanger inspection (which is critical — a cracked heat exchanger is a carbon-monoxide hazard and is generally a "replace the unit" situation).

If your furnace is more than 15 years old, recently failed a heat-exchanger inspection, has needed multiple repairs in recent winters, or is sized incorrectly for your home, we'll walk you through replacement options honestly. A properly sized 95–98% AFUE condensing furnace will typically cut gas bills by 20–35% versus an 80% AFUE unit, and the difference between a good installation and a bad one is dramatic — bad ductwork, oversized equipment and missing combustion-air provisions ruin even the best furnace.

Signs Your Furnace Needs Repair

  • Higher-than-normal gas bills with no change in usage
  • Burning, dusty or scorched smells when the heater runs
  • Banging, screeching or grinding sounds from the furnace area
  • Cold spots in rooms that used to heat evenly
  • The furnace fails to complete a full cycle and shuts off prematurely
  • The pilot light or burner flame is yellow instead of blue
  • Carbon-monoxide alarm activations near the heating equipment
  • Frequent breaker trips or thermostat resets

Heat Pumps & Mini Split Heat Pumps

A heat pump is essentially an air conditioner that runs in reverse — it can both cool your home in summer and heat it in winter using the same outdoor unit. Modern cold-climate heat pumps work efficiently down into single-digit temperatures and pair beautifully with a small backup electric or gas heater for the few days a year when Missouri sees a deep freeze. Heat pumps are typically 2–4 times more efficient than electric resistance heat and competitive with natural gas. We install ducted air-source heat pumps, ductless mini-split heat pumps and ground-source (geothermal) heat pumps for the customers who want the absolute lowest operating cost.

Hydronic Boiler Services

Hydronic systems heat water and circulate it through baseboard radiators, cast-iron radiators or in-floor radiant tubing. They're the gold standard for even, comfortable, draft-free heat — and when they're maintained properly they outlast multiple generations of forced-air systems. We install, service and replace gas-fired and electric boilers from Weil-McLain, Burnham, Lochinvar, Triangle Tube and Navien, plus radiant tubing systems for new builds and remodels.

FAQ — Heating

How often should I change my furnace filter?

Standard 1-inch fiberglass filters: every 30 days. Pleated filters: every 60–90 days. 4–5-inch media filters: every 6–12 months. A clogged filter is the single most common cause of furnace breakdowns we see.

Why does my heat pump blow cold air?

Heat pumps move heat rather than create it, so the air coming out of the registers is typically 90–105°F — warmer than room temperature but cooler than the 130–140°F you get from a gas furnace. That's normal. If the air is actually cold (below room temperature), the system is in defrost or there's a refrigerant problem.

What temperature should I set my thermostat?

68°F when home, 60–62°F at night and when away. Every degree below 70°F saves about 3% on heating costs.

Is my furnace giving off carbon monoxide?

You can't smell CO. The only way to know for sure is a properly placed CO detector and an annual combustion analysis by a qualified technician. We include CO testing on every heating service call.
